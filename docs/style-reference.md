# Style reference

Voices first: each row names the Japanese markers and the English devices that carry them. A line any speaker could
have said is a mistake. Paired examples follow (pilot batches b001-b003, approved by the controller's review).

## Voices
| Character | JP markers | English devices |
|---|---|---|
| Ryoma Sakamoto | Tosa dialect: 〜ぜよ, 〜じゃ, 〜がじゃ, わし, おんし/おまん, 〜きに, 〜ちゅう | loud, sunny, folksy swagger: "I tell ya", "y'know", "reckon", "ain't that somethin'", dropped g's now and then ("rockin'"), exclamations; first person "I" (never "this one"); calls people "pal", by name. Not a cowboy parody: one or two touches per line, not every word. |
| Shinsaku Takasugi | オレ様, 〜だろうが, 貴様, 〜してやる, 大福 lover | cocky and theatrical: "yours truly", "the great Takasugi", "you peasants", "obviously", short boasts; soft spot shown in grumbles. |
| Kogoro Katsura | polite です/ます, 〜ですね, 僕 (sometimes), inventor jargon | gentle, precise, a little prim; full sentences, "I believe", "if I may", "how fascinating"; gets flustered politely ("Wh-what?"). |
| Toshizo Hijikata | stern 〜だ, 〜しろ, 俺, 局長 devotion | clipped orders, few words: "Enough.", "Move.", "Don't make me repeat myself." Calls Kondo "the Commander". |
| Soji Okita | cool, sharp, sometimes sweetly menacing; 僕/俺 | cool and cutting; polite-sounding threats ("Shall I cut you down, then?"); idol stage voice when performing. |
| Yoshinobu Tokugawa | 余, 〜であるぞ, 〜せよ, ふはは | royal "We"/"I" with grandeur, imperious commands: "Bow before me.", "How amusing." |
| Naosuke Ii | 〜ですよ/〜でしょう with menace | silky, contemptuous politeness: "How very unfortunate for you." |
| Yataro Iwasaki | Tosa dialect, money-minded | fast-talking hustler: "big money", "leave it to me!". |
| Otose | motherly 〜だよ, 〜おくれ | warm landlady: "dear", "now, now", "you boys". |
| Narration (ト書き) | だ/である narration | plain past-tense narration, no slang. |
| Crowds, townsfolk | 〜だべ, 〜っす etc. | match the register: rustic ("ain't", "reckon"), rough ("ya", "dammit"). |

## Rules seen in review
- Keep names as the speaker uses them: Ryoma says "Takasugi", Hijikata says "Okita"; honorifics as in JP (D1).
- "雷舞" lines are about a live show: "our live", "the live"; "ロック" stays "rock".
- Song titles: as the game's own English, in quotes.
